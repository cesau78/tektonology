# Tektonology Church Maintenance Lifecycle

This lifecycle describes how Tektonology helps a church maintain its spaces, tools, and fixtures through a transparent, repeatable process powered by parishioner participation.

Each phase includes roles that parishioners can sign up for based on their gifts, time, and resources.

## Phase 1: Stewardship Funding

**Role: Philanthropists**

Philanthropists seed and sustain a dedicated maintenance fund.

- Contribute financial support to the maintenance pool.
- Enable predictable repair and replacement work instead of emergency-only fixes.
- Support a transparent ledger that is publicly viewable in an anonymous format.

## Phase 2: Issue Spotting and Reporting

**Role: Spotters**

Spotters identify maintenance needs and submit clear field reports.

1. Take a maintenance tag from a QR station.
2. Wrap or attach the tag to the broken item or area that needs attention.
3. Scan the tag's code with a phone.
4. Capture a photo and submit a short issue report.
5. Send the report into a review queue for approval and prioritization.

## Phase 3: Repair and Fabrication

**Role: Tektons**

Tektons provide the craftsmanship and technical labor needed to complete approved work.

- Evaluate approved requests and define the repair plan.
- Repair, replace, fabricate, or otherwise maintain the affected item.
- Document work completion so the maintenance history remains traceable.
- Return the maintenance tag to the QR station so it can be reused for future issues.

## Phase 4: Accountability and Continuity

**Shared Roles: Philanthropists, Spotters, Tektons**

All roles reinforce long-term sustainability.

- Funding activity remains transparent and trusted.
- Completed work is logged for future reference.
- New issues cycle back into the spotting phase.
- The church maintains a living, maintainable maintenance schedule.
